# Spec: items, item groups, editions and item parts

What a media is made of. A movie is one item; a series is items (its episodes) gathered in item groups (its seasons). Every media also has an edition, the content variant its items and files belong to, and a file reaches the items it plays through item parts. The HTTP payloads speak of seasons and episodes (rule 11); only the tables are generic, so a later media kind reuses them.

## Tables

1. `item_groups(id, "mediaId" NOT NULL, number, monitored, "preferredProvider", "posterUrl", overview, "airDate")`: the seasons of a series, `number` being the season number (`0` for specials). `FOREIGN KEY ("mediaId") REFERENCES media ON DELETE CASCADE`, `UNIQUE (id, "mediaId")`, index on `"mediaId"`.
2. `editions(id, "mediaId", family, label)`: one content variant of a media. `family` is a text under `CHECK (family IN ('video'))`, widened with each new kind of content. `FOREIGN KEY ("mediaId") REFERENCES media ON DELETE CASCADE`, `UNIQUE (id, "mediaId")`. A media has exactly one `video` edition; a second edition would be different content (a cut), never another encoding of the same one.
3. `items(id, "mediaId", "editionId", "groupId", number, "endNumber", title, overview, "airDate", runtime, monitored, "stillUrl", "markersScannedAt", "searchVector")`: what is watched. `"mediaId"` and `"editionId"` are `NOT NULL`; `"groupId"` and `number` are null for the item of a movie. `FOREIGN KEY ("editionId", "mediaId") REFERENCES editions (id, "mediaId")` and `FOREIGN KEY ("groupId", "mediaId") REFERENCES item_groups (id, "mediaId")`, both `ON DELETE CASCADE`: the database refuses an item whose edition or group belongs to another media. `CHECK ("groupId" IS NULL OR number IS NOT NULL)`. `UNIQUE (id, "mediaId")`, index on `"mediaId"`. `"endNumber"` is the last episode number a multi-episode file covers, kept as display metadata (`endEpisodeNumber`) and as the range the parts follow (rule 19).
4. The shape of a media follows `MediaKind::has_seasons`: a series has groups and grouped items and no ungrouped item; a movie has exactly one ungrouped item and no group. Nothing else enforces it, so every write that creates a media or changes its type keeps it (rules 6-8).
5. Season and episode ids are the group and item ids: the migration renamed the tables in place, so `seasonId` and `episodeId` of every payload, `season/<id>` and `episode/<id>` image paths and the ids held by `item_markers`, `subtitle_files`, `playback_states`, `playback_history`, `playlist_items`, `likes`, `content_recommendations`, `cleanup_*`, `watch_party_*` and `media_files` stayed valid. The item of a movie takes a fresh id from the same sequence.
6. Duplicate `(mediaId, number)` groups and `("groupId", number)` items are not constrained; lookups take the lowest id.

## Writes

7. `media::items::create_shell(conn, media, kind, want)` inserts the media's `video` edition, its want (rule 32) and, when the kind has no seasons, its item (`monitored = true`, everything else null). Every `INSERT INTO media` calls it in the same transaction: `POST /api/media`, the title import, the unmatched media a disk import creates, and the Radarr and Sonarr importers. A route that creates a media without it breaks rule 4.
8. A group is inserted with its `"mediaId"`; an item of a series with the `"groupId"`, the group's `"mediaId"` and the media's `video` edition (`media::items::video_edition`).
9. `PUT /api/media/:id` changing `type` follows it (`media::items::follow_kind`): a media that becomes a series loses its ungrouped item, and with it the progress and history of that item; a media that becomes a movie gains one if it has none. Groups and grouped items are kept.
10. Deleting a media removes its editions, groups and items by cascade.

## Reads and wire

11. The wire is unchanged (`wants` aside, rule 33): `seasonId` is the group id, `seasonNumber` the group `number`, `episodeId` the id of a grouped item, `episodeNumber` its `number`, `endEpisodeNumber` its `"endNumber"`. SQL that builds those payloads names the keys (`jsonb_build_object` or an alias); no query serialises a whole `items` or `item_groups` row.
12. An ungrouped item is not an episode. A query about episodes joins `item_groups` on `"groupId"` or filters `"groupId" IS NOT NULL`, so the item of a movie never appears in an episode list, count or lookup (the episode gates of `PATCH /api/media/episodes/:episodeId` and of the markers routes answer `404` for it).
13. Progress and history belong to an item: `playback_states` is unique on `(userId, itemId)` and `playback_history` rows carry an `itemId`, both cascading with the item. So are `playlist_items`, `watch_party_items`, `watch_party_proposals` and the live `watch_parties` row, which hold an `"itemId"` and no media or episode column; a party's chosen file stays on the row (`mediaFileId`). The SQL function `item_of(media, episode)` turns the wire pair `(mediaId, episodeId?)` into an item: an absent episode names the media's ungrouped item only when the media has exactly one (a media with two, such as a second edition, names none, rule 36), an episode id names that item when it belongs to the media, any other pair names none. A file carries no item: its items are its parts (rules 17-23).

## Versions and editions

14. Several files of one item are versions: the same content in other encodings, one picked at play time (a version is an ordered set of parts, rule 18). An edition is different content. This change creates one `video` edition per media and never splits a movie.

## Migration

15. The migration renames `seasons` to `item_groups` and `episodes` to `items` (with their sequences, keys and indexes), renames `seasonNumber` to `number` on groups and `seasonId`, `episodeNumber`, `endEpisodeNumber` to `"groupId"`, `number`, `"endNumber"` on items. It deletes the groups without a media and the episodes without a season (they were unreachable, and their markers and progress go with them by cascade), creates the editions (one per media), then gives every item its media and edition, inserts one ungrouped item per movie and adds the keys and checks above. The episodes keep their `"updatedAt"`.
16. There is no dual write and no view over the old names.

## Item parts

17. `item_parts("itemId", "mediaFileId", "mediaId", "version", position)`, primary key `("itemId", "mediaFileId")`, links a file to an item it plays. `FOREIGN KEY ("itemId", "mediaId") REFERENCES items (id, "mediaId")` and `FOREIGN KEY ("mediaFileId", "mediaId") REFERENCES media_files (id, "mediaId")`, both `ON DELETE CASCADE`: the database refuses a part whose file and item belong to different media, and a part goes with its file or its item. `CHECK (position >= 0)`, `CHECK ("version" >= 0)`, `UNIQUE ("itemId", "version", position)`, index on `"mediaFileId"`. `"version"` is `NOT NULL` with no default, so a writer states it. `media_files` carries `UNIQUE (id, "mediaId")` for the key.
18. A version is an ordered set of parts: the parts of one item sharing a `"version"` are the sequential pieces of that version, read in `position` order. A reader picks one version of the item, then plays its positions in order; parts of different versions are alternatives and are never mixed. A new file of an item is a new version at position `0` (the item's highest version plus one, `0` for the first), and a file keeps its version while it stays a part of its item. Grouping several files into one version (a book in several audio files) is written with books.
19. A file plays the item it was matched to and, when its name spans several episodes (`S01E02-E03`), every existing item of the same group numbered after the owner up to the last episode (`"endNumber"`). Missing items of the range are not created. When items are created, or a range is widened, the items inside it gain the parts of the range owner (`media::items::cover_ranges`). The range is stored on the owner item (`"endNumber"`), not on a file, so every part of the owner is spread, including a second version that is not a range. Each spread part is a new version of its inner item.
20. A movie's files are parts of its ungrouped item. A series file whose episode is unknown has no part: it stays listed in the media's files, unassigned. A media that becomes a movie gives its new item a part for every file of the media (rule 9).
21. An item has a file when it has a part (`EXISTS (SELECT 1 FROM item_parts WHERE "itemId" = items.id)`). The `hasFile` column and the range coverage subqueries do not exist; an inner episode of a multi-episode file reports `hasFile: true`, and is counted as downloaded, like its owner. Every read about files and watching uses all the items that have a part: the watched flag of lists and `watched-ids`, the next episode of Continue Watching, the episode scope of cleanup and the count of downloaded episodes.
    - Finishing a file completes every item it plays: when a heartbeat or an offline replay completes an episode, each other grouped item with a part on the same file is completed too (a state row with position `0`, a play in the history, removal from auto playlists), like the series and season toggles do.
    - A file is listed once where a list names a file through an episode (the playable entries of a party programme or playlist) and where an upgrade is searched (acquisition): only its first item counts, tested through the item parts (no other part of the file has a lower number), so the inner episodes of a range are not offered a second time.
22. `media_file_items("mediaFileId", "itemId", "episodeId")` is a read-only view giving each file with parts one item: the lowest-numbered one (ungrouped first, then by id), which is the owner of a range. `"episodeId"` is that item's id when it is grouped, `NULL` for a movie's item: it is the `episodeId` of every file payload, stream token, subtitle and job, null for a movie's files and for a series file without episode.
23. Replacing a file's parts (a re-ingested path, a rescan link) deletes its parts and inserts them again in one transaction (`media::items::attach`), each keeping the version it had on the same item. Writers of `item_parts` (`attach`, `cover_ranges`, the media type change, the provider season refresh that deletes items) take `pg_advisory_xact_lock(<item-versions class>, mediaId)` before anything else, so two of them never pick the same version and none deletes items while another holds the parts.

## Item parts migration

24. The item-parts migration creates the table, then inserts a part for every file linked to an item of its own media, a part for every existing item inside the range of an owner with `"endNumber"`, and a part on the ungrouped item of every movie file. Files linked to an episode of another media stay unassigned (counted in a notice). It then drops `media_files."episodeId"`, `items."hasFile"` and the `("groupId", "hasFile", "endNumber")` index (replaced by an index on `("groupId", number)`), and creates `media_file_items`.

## Markers

25. `item_markers` (`markers.md`, rule 1) holds two timelines. A marker without `"mediaFileId"` is on the item's timeline (`intro`, `outro`, `recap`, `credits`, detected on one file and applied to every version). A `chapter` always has a `"mediaFileId"` and is on that file's timeline, since versions of one item can be different cuts. `FOREIGN KEY ("itemId", "mediaFileId") REFERENCES item_parts ON DELETE CASCADE`: a chapter exists only for a part, and a removed part takes its chapters.
26. Chapters follow the probe by trigger, so no writer can forget them. `probe_chapters(streamInfo)` returns the usable entries of `streamInfo.chapters` in probe order (numeric `startSeconds >= 0` and `endSeconds > startSeconds`; anything else is skipped, never an error; an absent `title` is `NULL`, an empty one stays empty). `AFTER INSERT ON item_parts` copies the chapters of the file onto the new part. `AFTER UPDATE OF "streamInfo" ON media_files`, when `streamInfo->'chapters'` changed, replaces the chapters of every part of the file. A write of another `streamInfo` key leaves them. A file with no part has no chapter row.
27. Every part of a multi-episode file (the owner and each inner item) carries the file's chapters; several versions of one item each keep their own. Chapters ending past the file duration are kept. Chapter ids change whenever parts are replaced (rule 23) or the probe's chapters change; nothing exposes them.
28. The item-markers migration renames `episode_markers` to `item_markers` in place (marker ids stay valid), renames `episodeId` to `"itemId"` and `type` to `kind`, drops the rows with an unusable range (counted in a notice), adds the checks, the indexes and the two chapter triggers, and backfills the chapters of every existing part.

## Wants

29. `wants(id, "mediaId", family, language, "qualityProfileId", "minimumAvailability")` is one wished edition of a media. `family` is under `CHECK (family IN ('video'))`; `language` is the edition language, `NULL` for any, and is `NULL` for `video`. `UNIQUE NULLS NOT DISTINCT ("mediaId", family, language)` and that check make one `video` want per media. `FOREIGN KEY ("mediaId") REFERENCES media ON DELETE CASCADE`.
30. `"qualityProfileId"` is a profile of the want's family: `FOREIGN KEY ("qualityProfileId", family) REFERENCES quality_profiles (id, family) ON DELETE SET NULL ("qualityProfileId")`, so deleting a profile leaves the want with none (`profiles.md` rule 11). `"minimumAvailability"` is text, default `'released'`, with no `CHECK`: a value outside `announced`, `inCinemas`, `released` reads as `announced` (`plugins.md` rule 31).
31. `monitored` and `languageProfileId` stay on `media`: the first is the global switch, the second drives the audio and subtitle tracks (`subtitles.md`), not acquisition.
32. Every media has its `video` want from its creation: `media::items::create_shell` inserts one want per family of the kind (`MediaKind::families`), with the profile the import resolved and `minimumAvailability` at its default. A request keeps its own `qualityProfileId`; approving it writes that profile into the created media's video want.
33. The wire keeps `qualityProfileId`, `minimumAvailability` and the `qualityProfile` object on the media payload: they are read from the video want. `PATCH /api/media/:id/profiles`, and the bulk patch write the quality profile on the want (`media-write.md` rules 30, 31); `PUT /api/media/:id` does not persist it.
34. A want is satisfied when an edition of its family (and language) has a part at or above the profile's cutoff. For `video` this is the cutoff test of `media-query.md` rule 26 and of `GET /api/media/:id/tracking`; the acquisition candidates (`plugins.md` rule 31) are the wants not satisfied.

## Versions, strict item_of, one work per file

35. `playback_states."version"` is the version of the last file played, set by the `playback_states_version` trigger from the item's part of `"mediaFileId"` (`BEFORE INSERT OR UPDATE OF "mediaFileId"`): no writer sets it. It is kept when the file is deleted (`"mediaFileId"` becomes `NULL`) or is no longer a part. A reader that picks a file for an item takes, in order, the file the state names when it is still a part (`playback-state.md` rule 7), else the first part (lowest `position`) of the state's version, else of the item's highest version. A picker with no state (the mark-watched toggles, `toggle-watched` of a new row included) takes the first part of the highest version. `playback_states."positionSeconds"` is a position in the last file played: it is the version's position while every version is one file, and a multi-file version needs a position on the version's timeline (1.4). Resolving a file to the item it plays (`media_file_items`, stream resolve, playback-info, the bound-file check of the heartbeat, the party's `"mediaFileId"`) goes through the file's parts and is independent of versions.
36. `item_of(media, NULL)` answers only when the media has exactly one ungrouped item and is `NULL` otherwise, like an unknown pair. Every caller reads `NULL` as "no item" (rule 13): the wire pair never names one of several items by luck. Writers do not guess either: `attach` for a link without owner, the media type change and `sole_item` refuse a media with several ungrouped items. Routes that resolve a pair (`playback-state.md` rule 5, `playlists.md` add) answer `409 ambiguous_item` for a pair that names several ungrouped items, and keep their answer for an unknown pair. A party carries the stored `"itemId"` of the rows it reads (programme, playlist, proposals) and writes that, never going back through the pair. A client that must address one of several ungrouped items needs an `itemId` on the wire, which comes with the book clients.
37. Chapters stay per part (rules 25-27). A version made of several files needs chapters on the version's timeline (the file offsets added up), which books add with such versions; a version is one file and its chapters are its file's.
38. A file belongs to one work: `item_parts` requires the file and the item to share a media (rule 17). An omnibus (an EPUB holding four novels, a comics archive of several issues) is its own work, linked to the works it gathers through a collection whose entries carry `position` and `positionEnd` (`media-details.md` rule 17); it cannot play the items of the other works.
39. A kind CHECK (`media.type`, `requests."mediaType"`, `user_quotas.kind`, the kinds of `cleanup_rules.level` and `levels`) lists `MediaKind::ALL`, and a family CHECK (`editions.family`, `wants.family`, the quality families) lists the families `MediaKind::ALL` is wished in, a subset of `Family::ALL` that widens with the kind that first needs a family. A test finds the single-column CHECKs of those columns in `pg_constraint` and compares, so a new kind or family cannot miss one.

## Versions migration

40. `1789000000180_item-versions.sql` adds `item_parts."version"` (each existing part is its own version: `row_number() OVER (PARTITION BY "itemId" ORDER BY "mediaFileId") - 1`, since every part was at position `0`), the unique key on `("itemId", "version", position)`, and `playback_states."version"` backfilled from the part of the state's file (with `set_updated_at` disabled around it) and kept by its trigger. It redefines `item_of` (rule 36) and widens `collection_entries` (`media-details.md` rule 22).

## Tables keyed by group and item

41. `likes`, `content_recommendations`, `cleanup_candidates` and `cleanup_exclusions` hold `"groupId"` (FK to `item_groups`) and `"itemId"` (FK to `items`), both nullable, `ON DELETE CASCADE`, each with an index, next to `"mediaId"`. A row with neither is about the whole media (a movie, a series, a book); `"groupId"` is a group of the media (a season), `"itemId"` an item of it, grouped or not. `subtitle_files` holds `"itemId"` the same way, `ON DELETE SET NULL`. The wire keeps `seasonId` (the group) and `episodeId` (the item); today's routes write only grouped items, since a body names an item through `episodeId` and `episodeId` of an ungrouped item is not an episode (rule 12).
42. The migration renames `"seasonId"` to `"groupId"` and `"episodeId"` to `"itemId"` in place, with their constraints and indexes: season and episode ids are the group and item ids (rule 5), so no row changes and none is dropped.
